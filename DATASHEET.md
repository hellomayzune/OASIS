# 🏠 Dataset A — Room-level monitoring record (`NottinghamMeasured*.xlsx`)

## Motivation

**For what purpose was the dataset created?**

The dataset was created to provide continuous, hourly, room-level temperature and humidity records from an occupied dwelling across four consecutive cooling seasons. These records serve three primary analytical purposes within the workflow: conducting outdoor meteorological checks against MIDAS data (Stage 1), evaluating empirical overheating risk according to CIBSE TM59 criteria (Stage 2), and training and testing room-level surrogate models and baseline benchmark models (Stages 4–5).

**Who created the dataset?**

The dataset was assembled by the OASIS research team using a single case-study dwelling located in the East Midlands, UK. The property is the residence of co-author RT, who supplied the monitoring data and building metadata; to preserve research objectivity, RT did not participate in the analysis or interpretation of the data, which was performed independently by co-authors MZ and JW. The dataset was not created on behalf of any external commercial or institutional third party.

**Who funded its creation?**

No specific grant from a funding agency.

## Composition

**What do the instances represent?**

Each instance represents hourly measurements of air temperature (°C), relative humidity (%), and dew point (°C) recorded across eight monitoring locations within a four-bedroom detached house, comprising one outdoor site and seven indoor rooms. The dataset is organized into separate sheets corresponding to each location: `External`, `Kitchen_NW`, `Living_SE`, `Hall`, `Landing`, `MasterBed_SE`, `Bed4_SE`, and `Bed3_NW`. Sheet name suffixes denote principal room orientation, whereas circulation spaces (`Hall` and `Landing`) carry no directional suffix.

**How many instances are there?**

In the primary data file (`NottinghamMeasuredOASIS.xlsx`), each of the eight sheets contains 30,001 hourly records spanning from 1 May 2023 00:00 to 2 October 2026 00:00. The initial row of each sheet defines the standard column headers (`Time`, `Temperature`, `Humidity`, `Dew Point`); the analysis notebooks dynamically detect header positions to accommodate variations in title-row layout.

**Is it a sample?**

The dataset captures the complete time series of logged hourly records without downsampling. However, logger deployment was staggered: sensors in the `Hall` and `Bed4_SE` were installed after the initial deployment, resulting in approximately 1,490 fewer readings, concentrated predominantly at the beginning of the 2023 cooling season.

**What does each instance consist of?**

Each instance comprises raw data outputs directly exported from the physical loggers: timestamp (`Time`), dry-bulb air temperature (`Temperature`), relative humidity (`Humidity`), and calculated dew point temperature (`Dew Point`). All derived metrics, including exponentially weighted running means, CIBSE TM59 compliance indicators, and feature matrices, are generated programmatically within the analysis notebooks.

**Is there a label or target?**

The raw monitoring dataset contains no explicit target labels. Within the predictive workflows of Stages 4 and 5, individual indoor room temperatures (`T_in`) serve as target variables, or alternatively, hourly temperature differences serve as targets when training incremental temperature-change models.

**Is any information missing?**

Missing data is confined to the pre-installation period for the `Hall` and `Bed4_SE` loggers, alongside rare, isolated single-hour logging gaps. Detailed accounting of these missing records is provided in `stage2_missing_data_audit.csv`.

**Are relationships between instances explicit?**

All sheets share a unified temporal index defined by the `Time` column. Stage 2 pairs each indoor room record with concurrent `External` conditions. Stages 4 and 5 perform inner joins between each indoor space and the outdoor baseline, effectively aligning each room's analytical record to its respective sensor installation date.

**Are there recommended data splits?**

Chronological splits stratified by room are recommended. For Stage 4 model development, earlier complete monitoring years (1 May through 30 April) should be allocated to training, reserving the most recent complete year and subsequent partial periods for out-of-time evaluation. For Stage 5 recursive evaluation over annual horizons, an 80/20 chronological train–test split should be employed.

**Errors, noise or redundancy?**

Measurement uncertainty is bounded by logger hardware specifications. The local outdoor sensor systematically registers higher temperatures than the nearest Met Office weather station; the magnitude and character of this microclimatic discrepancy are formally quantified in the Stage 1 MIDAS comparison notebook.

**Self-contained?**

The dataset is self-contained. Analytical workflows evaluate these empirical records against external normative standards (CIBSE TM59, CIBSE TM52, and BS EN 16798-1), the text and parameters of which are referenced rather than redistributed.

**Confidential data?**

The dataset contains no confidential personal, health, or financial information. All spatial coordinates, exact street addresses, and occupant identities remain entirely anonymous throughout the repository.

## Collection process

**How was the data acquired?**

Data was directly measured via continuous physical monitoring. Indoor environment variables were recorded using Tinytag Ultra 2 data loggers (model TGU-4500), which feature a temperature resolution of 0.01 °C and a relative humidity resolution superior to 0.3% RH, with a rated accuracy of ±3.0% RH at 25 °C. Indoor loggers were positioned as close as practical to a nominal height of 1.10 m (the head of a seated person), with actual installation heights ranging from 0.70 m to 1.23 m due to dwelling spatial and occupancy constraints. Outdoor meteorological conditions were recorded using a weatherproof, IP68-rated Tinytag Plus 2 logger (model TGP-4500) engineered for an operating temperature range of −25 °C to +85 °C.

**Mechanism.**

Data logging was performed continuously and automatically at fixed one-hour intervals, eliminating manual transcription steps and associated human error.

**Timeframe.**

The monitoring period spans four consecutive cooling seasons from 2023 through 2026, targeting the standard CIBSE assessment window of 1 May through 30 September of each year.

**Who was involved?**

Data collection was managed by the OASIS research team, with field measurements supplied by the co-author residing in the property. The household maintained standard, unconstrained living routines and received no instructions or interventions regarding window operation, solar shading, or space heating; occupant presence and window opening behaviors were not actively recorded.

## Preprocessing / cleaning / labelling

In Stage 2, the workflow parses the `Time` timestamp and tags each record with room metadata, including room type, zone classification (Bedroom, Living Space, or Circulation), floor level, compass orientation, cooling season year, and CIBSE TM59 occupancy schedule flags. Each room record is subsequently joined to concurrent `External` environmental conditions. To preserve statistical integrity during CIBSE TM59 evaluation, any hours containing missing indoor or outdoor values are strictly excluded from both the numerator and the denominator of compliance calculations.

In Stages 4 and 5, environmental variables are renamed for predictive modeling (`T_in`, `T_out`, `RH_out`), and each room dataset is aligned to a uniform hourly grid without synthetic interpolation. Feature engineering pipeline steps construct predictive covariates including concurrent outdoor temperature and relative humidity, outdoor temperature lags (at 1, 3, 6, 12, and 24 hours), 24-hour and 48-hour rolling outdoor temperature means, the autoregressive previous-hour indoor temperature, and cyclic trigonometric encodings for hour-of-day and day-of-year. Records lacking a complete historical window for lag construction are truncated.

Throughout all preprocessing and feature transformation steps, the original raw monitoring workbooks (`NottinghamMeasured*.xlsx`) remain intact and unmodified within the `1_input/` directory.

## Uses

**Used for:**

The dataset is used in Stage 1 to compare local outdoor records against MIDAS weather data and to calculate comfort-hour distributions according to EN 16798-1 and ASHRAE Standard 55 adaptive comfort models. In Stage 2, it supports empirical overheating evaluations against CIBSE TM59 Criteria (a), (b) and (d), alongside Heat Index calculations, secondary overheating diagnostics, and operative-offset sensitivity analyses. In Stages 4 and 5, it enables the training, benchmarking, and evaluation of predictive models—including XGBoost, temperature-change (ΔT) XGBoost, ARX, 1R1C grey-box, and persistence baselines—as well as SHAP interpretability analysis, recursive multi-step forecasting, and extreme heatwave stress tests. Finally, the Stage 5 production models trained on the full record serve as surrogates for temperature projections under future weather (DSY2030 and DSY2080).

**Other possible uses:**

The dataset could also support evaluating alternative adaptive thermal comfort indices, benchmarking novel machine-learning or physics-informed indoor temperature forecasting architectures, and investigating within-dwelling diurnal thermal dynamics and inter-room microclimatic variability.

**Limits on future use.**

Because measurements originate from a single residential property, empirical findings and surrogate models constitute within-dwelling evidence and cannot be generalized or transferred directly to buildings with differing construction archetypes, orientations, occupancy profiles, or geographic locations. Furthermore, environmental logging was restricted to dry-bulb air temperature and relative humidity; mean radiant temperature, window opening behavior, mechanical ventilation rates, and real-time internal heat gain profiles were not measured.

**Should not be used:**

This dataset should not be interpreted as a statistically representative sample of the broader UK residential housing stock, nor should it be used as a formal, statutory compliance assessment under CIBSE TM59 or Building Regulations Approved Document O. Furthermore, these data must not be used in any attempt to reverse-engineer or identify the physical address of the case-study dwelling or its occupants.

## Distribution and maintenance

**Distribution.**

The primary raw dataset is **not publicly distributed** in order to honor participant confidentiality agreements and protect occupant privacy, as documented in architectural decision record `ADR-047`. However, all analytical outputs and processed data products derived from the raw dataset are openly released within the `2_output/` directory under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International license (CC BY 4.0). The codebase and data pipelines are fully standardized so that external researchers can run the notebooks on a dataset of the same structure.

**Maintenance.**

The dataset and accompanying codebase are maintained by the study authors. Supplementary monitoring years are integrated by depositing additional Excel workbooks following the `NottinghamMeasured*.xlsx` naming convention. The processing scripts for Stages 2, 4, and 5 dynamically discover and merge all matching files, automatically resolving overlapping timestamps by giving precedence to the most recently modified workbook. Stage 1 operates on the single workbook specified by the `OASIS_XLSX` setting in its configuration cell (defaulting to `NottinghamMeasuredOASIS.xlsx`).

# 🔬 Dataset B — Physics-model check dataset (`Nottingham_July2024_Validation.xlsx`)

## Motivation

Dataset B was created to evaluate the hour-by-hour alignment between the EnergyPlus / DesignBuilder dynamic thermal model and physical measurements within Stage 3A of the OASIS pipeline. This comparison bounds how far the physics-based scenario results (Stage 3B) can be relied on.

## Composition

**Instances.**

The dataset consists of a single worksheet named `10-30July_2024`, comprising 504 continuous hourly records covering the three-week period from 10 July to 30 July 2024. Cell A1 records the monitoring month, while the first row specifies column headers containing the day identifier (populated at the first hour of each day), hour of the day, and paired measured and simulated time series for six spatial locations (`Outdoor`, `Kitchen_NW`, `Living_SE`, `MasterBed_SE`, `Bed4_SE`, and `Bed3_NW`).

**Sample or complete?**

The dataset captures the entire 504-hour monitoring window without sub-sampling. This specific window represents the sole extended period during which the case-study dwelling remained unoccupied with all windows closed, effectively isolating building envelope thermal dynamics and solar gains from internal heat generation and occupant-driven ventilation.

**Label/target.**

The `Measured_*` columns serve as empirical ground-truth references against which the corresponding `Simulated_*` dynamic performance trajectories are evaluated.

**Missing data.**

The 504-hour time series contains no missing values. As a safeguard, Stage 3A performs and logs an automated data completeness audit upon loading the workbook.

**Splits.**

No data splits are applied because this dataset is used exclusively for deterministic model evaluation rather than parameter estimation or statistical model fitting.

## Collection process

Measured environmental series were harvested directly from the physical data loggers described in Dataset A. Simulated environmental series were generated using an EnergyPlus / DesignBuilder dynamic thermal model of the case-study building, forced by measured local outdoor temperatures. Model inputs—including envelope construction assemblies, thermophysical material properties, background infiltration rates, and internal loads—were systematically audited against as-built documentation. Simulation execution took place prior to and independent of the repository's notebook execution environment.

## Preprocessing

Notebook Stage 3A parses and reconstructs a continuous hourly DateTime index by forward-filling the day value and handling cells exported as full dates. The cleaned, index-aligned dataset is exported as `stage3a_validation_data_cleaned.csv`. No values are imputed, altered, or discarded during preprocessing.

## Uses and limits

**Used for:**

Dataset B is used to compute empirical error statistics—including Root Mean Square Error (RMSE), Coefficient of Variation of RMSE (Cv(RMSE)), Normalized Mean Bias Error (NMBE), Mean Absolute Error (MAE), and the coefficient of determination (R²)—for each monitored zone. These metrics are calculated alongside block-bootstrapped confidence intervals to assess performance against hourly calibration thresholds set by CIBSE TM63 and ASHRAE Guideline 14.

**Limits.**

The dataset covers five of the seven monitored zones, omitting circulation spaces (`Hall` and `Landing`), and spans a single, unoccupied three-week summer window with windows closed. Because occupied operating conditions, adaptive window opening behaviors, and extreme multi-day heatwave events are absent from this window, the physics-based model is formally characterized as checked against empirical measurements rather than fully validated across all operational regimes.

**Should not be used:**

This dataset should not be cited as evidence of thermal model accuracy outside the specified monitoring dates and zones, nor should it be used as a training dataset for empirical or machine-learning models.

## Distribution and maintenance

Dataset B is distributed directly within the repository's `1_input/` directory under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International license (CC BY 4.0) and is maintained by the study authors via repository issues and pull requests. Revised or updated simulation runs are incorporated by adding newly versioned input files rather than overwriting this baseline dataset.

# 🧱 Dataset C — Physics-model scenario output (`Nottingham_Simulated_Temperature.xlsx`)

## Motivation

Dataset C provides hourly simulated room temperatures for the as-built dwelling, five passive and fabric intervention scenarios, and four cardinal building orientations evaluated under future climate conditions. These simulation outputs serve as the analytical foundation for Stage 3B scenario assessments, support the physics-versus-machine-learning comparison in Stage 4, and inform the derivation of Passive and Hybrid temperature offsets in Stage 5.

## Composition

The dataset contains three worksheets, each comprising 8,760 hourly rows corresponding to an annual simulation cycle:

The `DSY2030` sheet structures temporal indexes (`time`, `month`, `day`, `hour`) alongside outdoor air temperature, followed by six distinct scenario blocks (`DSY30_Base`, `DSY30_1`, `DSY30_2A`, `DSY30_2B`, `DSY30_3A`, `DSY30_3B`) evaluated across six indoor thermal zones (`LIVING_SE`, `KITCHEN_NW`, `BED1_SE`, `BED2_NW`, `BED3_NW`, `BED4_SE`), yielding 41 columns.

The `DSY2080` sheet replicates this exact structural layout using `DSY80_*` scenario prefixes under 2080 future climate projections.

The `DSY30_Orientation` sheet evaluates the baseline as-built building envelope under DSY2030 weather when systematically rotated across four cardinal directions (`North`, `East`, `South`, `West`) across the same six thermal zones, comprising 29 columns.

The worksheet header structure uses row 1 to specify scenario or orientation labels merged across zone groups, while row 2 defines individual zone names. Timestamps use an hour-ending format (01:00–24:00). Within the thermal zones, `BED1_SE` corresponds to the Master Bedroom, whereas `BED2_NW` represents an unmonitored bedroom. The workbook's `Outdoor` column is empty; outdoor temperature is taken from the Dataset D weather files.

**Scenarios.**

| Code | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Base | As built: window U = 1.57 W/m²K, SHGC 0.69; 20% of window area open when indoor temperature exceeds 22 °C and closed when outdoor exceeds 26 °C; night purge on the upper floor, ground-floor windows closed at night |
| 1 | Increased ventilation (50% opening area) |
| 2A | Case 1 + internal blinds |
| 2B | Case 1 + external shutters |
| 3A | Passivhaus-level glazing (U = 0.78, SHGC 0.47) + Case 1 + internal blinds |
| 3B | Improved glazing (U = 1.34, SHGC 0.28) + Case 1 + external shutters |

All mitigation scenarios enforce standardized CIBSE TM59 profiles for internal heat gains from occupancy, lighting, and small power equipment.

**Label/target.**

No predictive labels or targets are specified, as this dataset consists exclusively of deterministic physics-based simulation outputs.

## Collection process

The dataset was generated by running the dynamic thermal model described in Dataset B under the forcing conditions of the future weather files detailed in Dataset D. Simulation runs were executed prior to notebook execution and outside this repository.

## Preprocessing

In Stage 3B, data pipelines reshape each worksheet into a standardized long format (`timestamp, group, room, temp_C, outdoor_C`), shift temporal indexes to an hour-beginning convention, and populate outdoor temperature series directly from the corresponding Dataset D weather CSV files. In Stage 5, scripts calculate room-, month-, and hour-specific temperature differentials between the baseline case (`Base`) and intervention scenarios `Case 2A` and `Case 3B`.

## Uses and limits

**Used for:**

Dataset C is used to evaluate CIBSE TM59 Criteria (a) and (b) across mitigation scenarios, orientations, and climate horizons in Stage 3B. It enables direct benchmark comparisons against machine-learning surrogate projections for spatial locations common to both models in Stage 4. Furthermore, it supports the calculation of Passive (Base − 2A) and Hybrid (Base − 3B) temperature offsets used during exposure screening in Stage 5.

**Limits.**

Simulated temperatures reflect standardized, idealized CIBSE TM59 operating conditions rather than real-world household occupant behavior. Because simulation parameters originate from a dynamic model checked over a single unoccupied monitoring window, findings represent comparative scenario evaluations rather than absolute empirical predictions of retrofit performance.

## Distribution and maintenance

Dataset C is distributed in the repository's `1_input/` directory under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International license (CC BY 4.0) and is maintained by the study authors via repository issues and pull requests. Re-simulated or updated scenarios are introduced by depositing newly named input files rather than altering this primary record.

# 🌍 Dataset D — Design Summer Year weather files (CSV)

`2030_Nottingham_a1fi_50p_DSY-hours.csv` and `Hour_50th_2080s_4600345_DSY.csv`

## Motivation

Dataset D provides third-party Design Summer Year (DSY) future climate files sourced from the COLBE platform (University of Bath). These files serve as outdoor meteorological boundary conditions for the dynamic EnergyPlus simulation scenarios in Stage 3 and as future climate drivers for the machine-learning surrogate projections in Stages 4 through 6.

## Composition

The dataset comprises two distinct future weather time series:

The `2030_Nottingham_a1fi_50p_DSY-hours.csv` file represents projected meteorological conditions for the 2030s under the High Emissions (A1FI) scenario at the 50th percentile (station header ID 4550350).

The `Hour_50th_2080s_4600345_DSY.csv` file represents projected meteorological conditions for the 2080s at the 50th percentile (station header ID 4600345).

Each CSV file contains 8,764 total rows, consisting of a station identification header line, a surface tilt/bearing/ground-reflectance metadata line, a column name row, a measurement units row, and 8,760 hourly data records. Each record specifies temporal indices (`month`, `day`, `hour` on an hour-ending 1–24 convention) alongside meteorological variables including dry-bulb temperature, wet-bulb temperature, dew point, relative humidity, solar radiation, illuminance, wind dynamics, and sky-cover parameters. The analysis notebooks selectively extract dry-bulb temperature and relative humidity.

## Preprocessing

Data pipelines verify month and day ordering before aligning rows to an hour-beginning index across a standard non-leap reference year. In Stage 4, the initial 48 hours of each series serve as a feature warm-up window. In Stage 5, the weather series is padded circularly by 48 hours solely to build lag and rolling features; no physical continuity is implied. In Stage 6, stress-testing workflows apply uniform scalar increments ranging from +1 °C to +3 °C across the dry-bulb temperature series to evaluate model response under amplified heat hazard conditions.

## Uses and limits

**Used for:**

Dataset D provides boundary condition forcing for EnergyPlus dynamic thermal models and serves as input data for surrogate model projections. Additionally, it supports feature extrapolation audits against historical training ranges in Stage 4, as well as hazard characterization and the uniform-warming stress test in Stage 6.

**Limits.**

Because Dataset D consists of two discrete single-realization weather years (each specifying one emissions pathway and one percentile level), these files represent specific standardized exposure scenarios rather than a full probabilistic characterization of future climate uncertainty.

## Distribution and maintenance

These weather files are third-party data products and are **not redistributed** within this repository. Researchers must acquire the original files directly from the COLBE platform ([colbe.bath.ac.uk](https://colbe.bath.ac.uk/)) subject to its terms of use, placing the unmodified CSV files into the `1_input/` directory using the filenames specified above.

# 📡 Dataset E — Met Office MIDAS Open hourly observations (CSV)

`midas-open_uk-hourly-weather-obs_dv-202607_nottinghamshire_00554_sutton-bonington_qcv-1_<year>.csv` for 2023, 2024, and 2025

## Motivation

Dataset E provides official regional meteorological records from the nearest Met Office weather station to check the on-site outdoor monitoring series (Dataset A, `External`). This check matters because the outdoor running mean temperature directly establishes the adaptive thermal comfort limit of CIBSE TM59 Criterion (a).

## Composition

The dataset comprises hourly meteorological observations recorded at Met Office Station 00554 (Sutton Bonington) formatted in standard BADC-CSV structure. Each annual file contains a metadata header block terminated by a `data` tag, followed by hourly observation rows and an `end data` marker. Key variables extracted by the analysis pipelines include timestamp (`ob_time`), dry-bulb air temperature (`air_temperature`), temperature quality control flag (`air_temperature_q`), relative humidity (`rltv_hum`), and dew point (`dewpoint`). The files reflect dataset release version `dv-202607` and quality-control version 1.

## Preprocessing

In Stage 1, data pipelines automatically discover and ingest all files matching the pattern `midas-open_uk-hourly-weather-obs*.csv`, eliminate duplicate timestamps, and reindex records onto a continuous hourly temporal grid. Observations flagged with quality code 106 are cataloged and retained. Because the logger's timestamp convention is not documented, the shift that maximises the cross-correlation between the two series is found and applied to the measured series before hourly comparison (MIDAS is in UTC).

## Uses and limits

**Used for:**

Dataset E supports hourly, daily, and rolling-mean temperature comparisons against the on-site outdoor logger in Stage 1. It quantifies microclimatic discrepancies and evaluates their downstream impact on EN 16798-1 and ASHRAE Standard 55 adaptive comfort boundaries and cumulative comfort-hour tallies.

**Limits.**

Data originate from a single, open, grass-surface agricultural station situated approximately 12 km from Nottingham. Consequently, local microclimatic urban heat island effects cannot be formally decoupled from sensor-shielding or placement artifacts using these two time series alone.

## Distribution and maintenance

These meteorological records are third-party data products and are **not redistributed** within this repository. Met Office MIDAS Open data are published by the Centre for Environmental Data Analysis ([archive.ceda.ac.uk](https://archive.ceda.ac.uk/)) under the Open Government Licence, which mandates appropriate attribution citing the specific CEDA dataset version. Users should download the annual files and place them into the `1_input/` directory; the Stage 1 notebook dynamically parses all matching `midas-open_uk-hourly-weather-obs*.csv` files.

## Constraints carried into every derived table

Cumulative hours exceeding 26 °C, Cooling Degree-Hours (CDH), screening risk tiers (Low ≤ 1%, Elevated > 1–3%, High > 3–10%, and Severe > 10% of cooling-season hours above 26 °C), and associated confidence flags represent methodological screening constructs specific to this study; they do not constitute formal compliance classifications under CIBSE TM59 or Building Regulations Approved Document O.

Cooling Degree-Hours (CDH) serve strictly as a temperature-based thermal demand proxy expressed in °C·h and are not converted into active cooling energy consumption, carbon emissions, or financial costs.

Passive and Hybrid intervention results are generated through cross-model sensitivity experiments that transfer relative temperature reductions from EnergyPlus simulations onto machine-learning surrogate projections; consequently, they should be interpreted as screening-level estimates rather than direct measurements of installed intervention performance.

Future climate DSY results generated by the surrogate models represent projections of the monitored household's specific operational patterns under future weather forcing, and tend to systematically under-predict peak indoor temperatures during the hottest hours.

# 📌 Cross-cutting notes

**Single-dwelling scope.**

All datasets within this repository describe a single four-bedroom detached property of traditional masonry construction situated in the East Midlands, UK. The building envelope aligns with the 2021 Building Regulations Part L standard, featuring a design air permeability of 6.2 m³/h·m² at 50 Pa, approximately 45 cm roof eaves projections across all façades, top-hung window units with a maximum opening limit of approximately 45°, and acoustic glazing installations in bedroom spaces. Specific envelope thermal transmittance values (U-values) applied throughout the analytical framework comprise external walls at 0.25 W/m²K, ceiling to loft at 0.12 W/m²K, and glazing assemblies at 1.57 W/m²K.

**Personal data.**
